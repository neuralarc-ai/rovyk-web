Rovyk palette.
Monochrome, with four signals.

The neutral ramp does all the structural work: surfaces, borders, type, every card and panel on the site. The four brand hues never build layout. They mark state, and they appear in marks small enough to read as punctuation.

The four signals

Each hue owns one meaning and never borrows another's. Base is the mark colour; text is the lightened variant that clears 4.5:1 on black; tint and edge are the only fills allowed.

Where each one lands

The mapping is semantic, not decorative. If a component does not express one of these four states, it stays monochrome.

The 90/10 rule

Colour is rationed by area, not by taste. Two to four percent of any screen carries hue. The primary download button and the orb are never part of it.

✓ Colour is allowed here

  • Marks under 16px. Status dots, task ticks, the wake indicator, a 1px underline.
  • Tints at 12–18%. Confirmation panels, the memory glyph, a hovered state chip.
  • Small glyph fills. The dot matrix inside the memory tile, a status LED in a mock, an icon that expresses state rather than labelling a thing.
  • Focus rings. Indigo, everywhere, on every interactive element.
  • One hue per screen height. Two brand hues never sit in the same component.

✕ Colour stays out of here

  • Section backgrounds. Surfaces are neutral, always. The hero photograph is the single large colour field on the page.
  • Headlines and body copy. Type is white or one of the ink alphas.
  • The primary CTA. Download stays white on black; nothing may compete with it.
  • Card borders and dividers. Those are line alphas, not hue.
  • The orb. It stays monochrome white. The product's face is neutral, so hue always reads as state and never as decoration.
  • Above the fold. The hero photograph is the only colour up there. No brand hue appears in the hero except inside the device mock.
  • Gradients between two brand hues. Reads as a template, not a system.

A rough budget for a full viewport: neutral ground, a small share of ink, and a sliver of hue. When a screen exceeds it, something is being decorated rather than signalled.
